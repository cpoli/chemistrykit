Oscillating reactions
-----------------------

Chemical mechanisms that oscillate instead of relaxing to equilibrium:
the Brusselator limit cycle, Lotka's autocatalytic scheme with its
neutral orbits, and the Oregonator model of the Belousov-Zhabotinsky
reaction.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Field and Noyes (1974) boiled the FKN mechanism of the Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction -- cerium-catalyzed oxidation of malonic acid by bromate -- down to three intermediates: HBrO\ 2 (x), bromide (y), and the oxidized catalyst Ce(IV) (z). The resulting &quot;Oregonator&quot; (~chemistrykit.kinetics.systems.oscillators.Oregonator) produces the BZ reaction&#x27;s hallmark relaxation oscillations: long quiet stretches while bromide is slowly consumed, then an autocatalytic burst of HBrO\ 2 that oxidizes the catalyst (the colour flash seen by Belousov), which in turn regenerates bromide and shuts the burst off. Raising the stoichiometric factor f well above 2 stabilizes the steady state and the oscillation disappears.">
