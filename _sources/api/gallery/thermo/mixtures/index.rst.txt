Mixtures and colligative properties
------------------------------------

Raoult's-law P-x-y diagrams, Henry's law for dilute solutes, van't Hoff's
osmotic pressure and the colligative properties, and the Margules,
Wilson, NRTL and UNIQUAC activity-coefficient models for non-ideal
solutions: the ethanol-water azeotrope, liquid-liquid phase splitting,
and the combinatorial/residual decomposition.

Adding the two lines (Dalton&#x27;s law) gives the total pressure, and BinaryIdealSolution computes the vapor composition, which is always richer in the more volatile component. That enrichment is the basis of fractional distillation.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Margules wrote the activity coefficients of a binary liquid as power series in mole fraction. The one-parameter form used by MargulesSolution, \ln\gamma_1 = A x_2^2 and \ln\gamma_2 = A x_1^2, turns Raoult&#x27;s straight lines into curves: A &gt; 0 (unlike molecules repel) bows the total pressure upward and can produce a maximum-pressure azeotrope, while A &lt; 0 bows it downward. The model still obeys the Gibbs-Duhem relation, and each component tends to Henry&#x27;s law at infinite dilution.">

With just two parameters it describes strongly non-ideal miscible mixtures. Below, at 70 degC, pure-component vapor pressures come from Antoine&#x27;s equation (NIST constants), and Wilson parameters of the size fitted to ethanol-water data give the familiar maximum-pressure azeotrope near 90 mol% ethanol. That azeotrope is why ordinary distillation cannot purify ethanol past about 95 wt%.">
